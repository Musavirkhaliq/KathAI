# Abstract

Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) for the Kashmiri language (*Kōshur*, ISO 639-3: `kas`)—a Dardic language spoken by over 7 million people—has historically remained constrained by severe corpus fragmentation, intricate Perso-Arabic central vowel orthography, and acute under-representation in multilingual foundation models. In this work, we present a comprehensive, reproducible, and scientifically audited systematic study of Kashmiri automatic speech recognition. 

First, we aggregate, clean, and standardize **214.65 hours (106,487 audio recordings)** of native Kashmiri speech across 12 heterogeneous sources, establishing strictly speaker-disjoint partitions and an audited **159-token Character CTC Tokenizer** that natively encodes contrastive central vowels (`ٲ`, `ؠ`, `ۆ`, `ۄ`, `ێ`, `ۍ`, `ؽ`) with a verified **0.000% out-of-vocabulary (OOV)** rate. 

Second, we conduct controlled empirical fine-tuning of self-supervised multilingual encoders (`facebook/wav2vec2-xls-r-300m`). We demonstrate that acoustic scaling from 92.9h (B-01) to 165.3h (B-02C) reduces validation Word Error Rate (WER) from **33.86% to 27.84%** (greedy) and **25.24%** with KenLM language model shallow fusion ($p < 0.0001$). 

Third, through an exhaustive reproducibility and evaluation integrity audit, we uncover a critical sample-rate mismatch in legacy evaluation pipelines: ingesting 22,050 Hz audio unresampled into 16,000 Hz acoustic models artificially inflated historical error rates by ~3% absolute. Applying mathematically rigorous Kaiser windowed sinc resampling instantly eliminates **493 phantom word errors** on the 1,001-utterance diagnostic assessment split (`dev_assessment.jsonl`, 16,845 words), lowering KenLM WER from **27.39% (4,614 errors) to 24.46% (4,121 errors)** on frozen weights. Crucially, this audit establishes that foundation model B-02C and sequence-continuation model E-03 are statistically indistinguishable, differing by **exactly one word error** across 16,845 words ($p = 0.963$). 

Fourth, we investigate post-foundation acoustic optimization paradigms—including synthetic multi-condition noise augmentation, progressive layer unfreezing, intermediate CTC supervision, and noisy pseudo-labeling—reporting transparent negative findings and proving that short-horizon interventions fail to surpass converged full-corpus acoustic representations. 

Finally, we analyze the search space and decoding dynamics: restoring beam width 16 with history pruning eliminates an 84-word search penalty, while KenLM calibration ($\alpha=0.20, \beta=-0.50$) and Candidate-Relative Sequence Ranking modestly lower errors to **24.27%–24.30%** ($p = 0.0847$). An $N$-best oracle analysis reveals an oracle WER ceiling of **19.23% (3,240 errors)**, demonstrating that 881 word errors (21.4% of total errors) represent recoverable search/scoring headroom, while 78.6% require acoustic scaling. We release our complete code provenance, audited manifests, and replication pipelines to establish a rigorous scientific benchmark for Dardic speech processing.
